# GENIUS Act — reserve, disclosure & audit provisions

Source: GENIUS Act, Public Law 119-27 (119th Congress, S.1582).
Authoritative text: https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1582/text
Working extract of the provisions relevant to attestation analysis — verify
against the authoritative statutory text before relying on a precise quote.

## Sec. 4(a)(1) — Reserve requirements

A permitted payment stablecoin issuer shall maintain identifiable reserves
backing outstanding payment stablecoins on an at least one-to-one basis,
with the fair value of reserves equalling or exceeding the par value of
outstanding stablecoins at all times. Reserves may consist only of: U.S.
coins and currency, Federal Reserve notes, or funds held at a Federal
Reserve Bank; demand deposits or insured shares at insured depository
institutions; Treasury bills, notes, or bonds with a maturity of 93 days or
less; repurchase agreements backed by short-term Treasuries; reverse
repurchase agreements collateralised by Treasury instruments; securities of
registered government money market funds investing in the foregoing;
similarly liquid Federal-Government-issued assets approved by regulators;
or tokenized forms of the preceding reserve assets. Reserve assets shall be
segregated, not commingled with the issuer's operational funds, and shall
not be rehypothecated.

## Sec. 4(a)(1)(B) — Redemption policy disclosure

Issuers must publicly disclose the issuer's redemption policy, which shall
establish clear and conspicuous procedures for timely redemption, subject
to limitations only as permitted by regulators. Fees associated with
purchasing or redeeming stablecoins must be publicly, clearly, and
conspicuously disclosed in plain language, and may be changed only with not
less than 7 days' prior notice to consumers.

## Sec. 4(a)(1)(C) — Monthly reserve composition reporting

Issuers must publish, monthly, the composition of the issuer's reserves on
the issuer's website, containing the total number of outstanding payment
stablecoins issued by the issuer, and the amount and composition of the
reserves — including the average tenor and the geographic location of
custody of each category of reserve instruments.

## Sec. 4(a)(3) — Audit and certification requirements

The monthly reserve information must be examined by a registered public
accounting firm. The chief executive officer and chief financial officer of
the issuer must certify the accuracy of the reported information to
regulators each month. False certifications carry criminal penalties
consistent with section 1350(c) of title 18, United States Code. Issuers
with more than $50 billion in consolidated total outstanding issuance must
additionally publish annual audited financial statements.
