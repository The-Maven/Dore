# BIS — Stablecoin growth: policy challenges and approaches (Bulletin No 108, July 2025)

Bank for International Settlements analysis. Source:
https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull108.htm

## Key takeaways

> Stablecoins' linkages with the traditional financial system are growing,
> which raises policy challenges ranging from preserving financial
> integrity to mitigating financial stability risks.

> Broader use of foreign currency-denominated stablecoins could raise
> concerns about monetary sovereignty and, in some jurisdictions, erode
> the effectiveness of existing foreign exchange regulations.

> The principle of "same risks, same regulation" faces limitations in the
> context of stablecoins, highlighting the need for tailored regulatory
> approaches that address the nature and specific features of stablecoins.

## Reserve backing — the typology

Fiat-backed stablecoins hold heterogeneous portfolios of reserve assets,
with most holding a mix of reverse repos, publicly-issued debt securities
(predominantly short-dated US Treasuries) and cash or cash equivalents —
in different proportions across issuers. The reserve asset pool backing
the stablecoins in circulation, and its capacity to meet redemptions,
underpins the issuer's peg promise.

Three main variants for backing exist: fiat-denominated short-term assets
(the dominant model for payment stablecoins), crypto collateral, and
algorithmic arrangements. Algorithmic stablecoins have repeatedly failed
to hold their peg under stress; the policy debate has converged on the
fiat-backed model with prudential standards on the reserve.

## Linkages to traditional finance

Stablecoins are no longer self-contained crypto plumbing. Holdings of
short-term US Treasuries by the largest issuers run into the tens of
billions; redemption pressure transmits into Treasury markets and the
repo market. Banks act as custodians of reserve cash; some banks issue
their own tokenised deposits or stablecoins. As linkages grow, the
financial-stability case for prudential oversight strengthens.

## Run risk

A stablecoin's value rests on holders' belief that redemption at par will
be honoured. That belief is sensitive to the composition and liquidity of
the reserve, to disclosure quality, and to the operational reliability of
the redemption channel. A loss of confidence — driven by reserve
opacity, redemption friction or a peer-issuer failure — can produce a
run that forces fire sales of reserve assets. The empirical record shows
how rapidly an event at one issuer can spread.

## Policy approaches

As of 2023, more than 60% of responding jurisdictions had or were
developing regulatory frameworks for stablecoins. Common features:
licensing of issuers, asset-backing requirements, disclosure of reserve
composition, redemption rights at par, segregation of reserve assets and
prudential standards on the issuer's balance sheet. Jurisdictions differ
on whether stablecoin issuance is restricted to insured depository
institutions, on the treatment of foreign-currency-denominated issuance,
and on how cross-border arrangements should be supervised.
